Step Sequences
What is considered as a “small pattern” for reduction of GOE?
If the StSq utilizes only part of the ice surface, the GOE reduction is -1 to -3.
If the StSq is only partly performed to the music, how will this affect the GOE?
GOE reduction of “partly or fully does not correspond to the music” -1 to -4 applies. This element cannot start higher than +2 in the GOE evaluation.
A skater performs a wonderful step sequence with deep and clean steps and turns, to the music with good energy, ﬂow and with an interesting pattern on the ice. At one point of the sequence there is a small loss of control and stumble. How will this affect the GOE?
The final GOE reflects the whole element overall so both positive aspects and negative aspects should be considered. The positive bullet for “effortless throughout with good energy, flow and execution” cannot be awarded, so the maximum starting GOE is +3 for this element before the reduction is taken for the small stumble. If there is any kind of mistake that requires a reduction, the starting GOE cannot be higher than +3.
In the FS during the StSq the skater performs a jump element with more than one revolution and falls. What will the call be and how will the GOE be evaluated?
The Technical Panel will call StSq “F” and jump element “F”. Both elements will have a GOE reduction of -5 for the Fall. The deduction is 1.0 point in total for one fall (Junior and Senior categories, see Novice Communication for deductions for Novice).

What does the new reduction “Does not highlight choreography” mean?
The chosen movements in the Choreographic Sequence should reflect and support the composition of the program. If the movements are not reflecting or highlighting the choreography, the GOE reduction is -1 to -3.

Jump Elements, Throw jumps
If the 1Eu is executed as a step over, how will this affect the GOE?
GOE reduction of “Euler executed as step over” - 1 to -3 applies.
If there is a full revolution on the ice in between a jump combo or jump sequence, how will this affect the GOE?
GOE reduction of “2 three turns in between jump combo/seq” -2 to -3 applies.
If a skater executes a weak landing of a jump with a hop and the Technical Panel determines it is not a jump, how will this affect the GOE?
GOE reduction of “weak landing” -1 to -3 applies.
If a landing of a jump is on the other foot, how will this affect the GOE?
In combinations/sequences all jumps with more than one revolution, except the last jump may be landed on either foot. If the skater lands the last jump on the wrong foot, the GOE shall be reduced by “weak landing” -1 to -3.

Program Components
In pair skating, if both skaters fall in an element, for example in the solo jump, is this considered as two serious errors?
Yes, this is two serious errors and consequently the maximum score for all Program Components is 8.75.
If there is an interruption in the program due to an adverse condition not related to the skater, e.g., music problems, lights, ﬁre alarm etc, how will this affect the Program Components?
If there is an interruption in the skater’s performance due to a reason not related to the skater, this should not affect the Program Components score.
One pair team displays a difference in their Skating Skills as the man is not as strong as the woman. How will this affect the mark of Skating Skills?
In pair skating, there must be equal demonstration of the criteria by the skaters. If there are notable differences between the skaters, the mark should reﬂect the unit as a whole, e.g., ﬁnd the middle mark between the skaters.
Why is Originality no longer one of the evaluation criteria for Program Components?
The deﬁnition of the word “originality” includes being new, special or interesting, different from anything that has been created before, novel and unusual. It is not possible to always be “new” in all programs, in all competitions, in every season. Originality can be referred to any criteria of the three components:
In the way of skating, In the way of moving, In the personal and particular use of the arms, head, hands etc, In the patterns, In the way of connecting the elements, In the design of the movements.
Original can be the way of combining or associating different pieces of music or styles. Original can be the way of translating music

When judging Skating Skills in pairs, the skating repertoire of steps, turns and skating movements with blade and body control should be executed with similar technique. will this affect the Program Components mark?
When there are several mistakes in a program that affects the ﬂuidity and continuity of the program, how much should the Program Components marks be reduced with?
If a program contains 2 or more Serious errors, the maximum score for each program component is 8.75; this is if the program as a whole is still deemed to be “Excellent or Outstanding”. When evaluating a skating program the judge should be “building the mark” during the performance where the mark will be increased and/or decreased during the time of skating. Guidelines for Referees how to analyze/check results
These guidelines can also be used for judge’s own self-evaluation and education.
COMPONENTS It is more important to look at a judge’s marks vertically throughout the competition rather than comparing the marks of all judges from the same skater.
DIFFERENCES OF COMPONENTS BETWEEN DIFFERENT SKATERS àhas the judge showed the differences in the same component between different skaters? If the composition of one skater has been good and for the other skater fair, has the judge given 7 for the ﬁrst skater and 4 for the other? à is there enough big difference between those marks to reﬂect the difference in the component? à how big of a scale has the judge had between the lowest and the highest same component? à If in the competition there has been a wide variety of skating skills between the skaters, has the judge shown this in their marks or is the difference too small?
DIFFERENCES IN THE 3 COMPONENT MARKS OF THE SAME SKATER OF ONE JUDGE à does the same component always tend to be the highest component (e.g. SS) à are the different components of one skater always in the same relation to each other? For example: Skater 1 CO=5.50, PR=5.25, SS 5.00 Skater 2 CO=6.0, PR= 5.75, SS 5.50 Skater 3 CO=4.50, PR=4.25, SS 4.00 In this example the judge has always given the highest mark for composition, second highest presentation and lowest skating skills. In addition, the relation between the marks is always the same within 0.25. àis there ever a bigger difference between the different components, e.g. more than 1 point? àhas the judge tried to show differences in between the different components of the same skater?
DO THE COMPONENT MARKS REFLECT THE LEVEL OF EACH COMPONENT? àhas the best composition of the competition received the highest CO marks from the judge? àhas the skater with the weakest skating skills received the lowest SS marks from the judge?


àdo the marks reﬂect the skills shown that day? If composition was weak, has the judge given a mark around 3 etc.
Competition with SP and FS àhas the judge evaluated the skills of the skater so that the starting order or the result of the previous segment has not affected the components? For example, the skater in the ﬁrst Free Skating warm-up group has one of the best compositions in the competition – has the judge given one of the best composition marks for this skater or is the mark too low?
